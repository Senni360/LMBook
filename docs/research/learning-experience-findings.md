# Establishing the right learning task before creating audio

Research findings and decision memo · 2026-09-14

**Recommendation:** develop the owner's five required preparation areas into a conversation that resolves consequential ambiguity and ends with a shared episode brief. Keep the number of follow-ups responsive to what is still unclear. Treat this as a candidate ready for rehearsal, with learning effectiveness still to be evaluated.

The central requirement is to establish what the listener needs the material to become. A complete annotated reading, an explanation of a syllabus, and help performing a calculation impose different obligations. “Detailed audio overview” does not distinguish them. More engaging hosts or a stronger generic prompt cannot reliably repair that missing decision afterward.

This review supports the proposed direction without establishing that five questions are optimal, that mandatory preparation improves learning by a particular amount, or that this candidate will work reliably in use. The accompanying [candidate conversation](learner-interview.md) makes the recommendation concrete. The [learning evidence memo](learning-evidence.md) records study methods, limitations, and counterevidence. The [research plan](learning-experience-plan.md) remains the place for the broader programme and outstanding work.

## 1. The needs that should govern the decision

The owner wants sustained learning value for themselves and friends and accepts preparation effort when it improves that value. Their example of ten hours was an expression of that priority. It was not a duration requirement, a measured return, or evidence that longer work necessarily produces a better result.

The following needs come from the conversation, rather than from experiments with the app:

| Reported situation | What success requires | A plausible output that would still fail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| German literature: stories and concepts in a substantial course document | The requested original text, in sequence, with clearly separated explanatory pauses | A thorough, accurate paraphrase that removes the wording the learner needs to hear |
| Roman Empire: a chapter plus defined learning requirements | Explanations that meet the actual requirements, including their requested depth and operations | An entertaining general history, or reading every sentence without explaining the required ideas |
| Economics: familiar definitions but difficulty answering numerical questions | Support for recognizing the task, choosing the relevant relationship and quantities, carrying out steps, and interpreting the result | Another clear definition of GDP or unemployment that leaves the first calculation step unexplained |
| Politics for personal interest | A useful question or scope, with room for deeper connections and accurately represented uncertainty | Inventing exam requirements or forcing every disagreement into a two-sided debate |
| Listening on walks | Spoken material that remains followable, with any visual dependence made explicit | Requiring a phone glance at every step without establishing that the learner wants that experience |
| Biology for other learners | A provisional framework that can absorb their needs | A settled biology teaching style inferred from the owner's economics experience |

Three decisions must remain separate: **coverage**, **treatment of original wording**, and **the activity the learner should be able to perform**. A learner can require all relevant ideas while permitting rephrasing, or require particular passages verbatim while asking for selective surrounding explanation. These are independent commitments; a single depth setting cannot represent them.

Subject and purpose are also independent. History and politics may share source-handling principles, but a subject label should not decide whether the learner wants exploration, assessment preparation, or annotated reading. Shared preparation can precede subject-specific teaching guidance without prematurely combining the subjects into one uniform lesson style.

## 2. What the evidence changes

The relevant literature spans survey interviewing, information seeking, mathematics instruction, text learning, spoken explanations, and podcast listening. These settings illuminate individual decisions. None establishes the effectiveness of the complete SenniBook experience.

### Clarification can uncover needs people do not volunteer

Conrad and Schober's research report describes survey experiments in which clarification particularly helped respondents whose situations did not map straightforwardly onto a question. Request-only help could miss difficulties, and conversational clarification also cost time. These were defined survey tasks, not learning-app interviews. The product inference is to surface consequential ambiguity proactively, without assuming either that unrequested help is unnecessary or that every answer warrants further probing. [Conrad & Schober, 1999](https://www.bls.gov/osmr/research-papers/1999/pdf/st990250.pdf)

In an online search study with 89 participants and four tasks, the quality of clarification questions affected usefulness and disturbance; selectively presenting good questions compared favourably with always presenting the available questions. The questions were prepared and checked in advance, and participants could search without answering them. This supports scrutinizing question relevance, but does not establish the value of mandatory AI-generated preparation. [Zou et al., 2023](https://irlab.science.uva.nl/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/zou2022asking.pdf)

**Recommendation:** retain the five required areas, but justify every follow-up by the decision its answer can change. “We have asked five questions” is a completion count. “We agree what must be included and how it should be taught” is the intended outcome.

### Knowing a definition does not settle what help a calculation needs

Experiments with decimal learning show interacting conceptual and procedural development. Small algebra studies also suggest that a next-step performance sample can inform adaptation, while exposing reliability and generalization limits. These findings support investigating the particular difficulty; they do not validate broad learner labels or a diagnosis from one answer. [Rittle-Johnson et al., 2001](https://siegler.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/2001-Siegler-Alibali.pdf), [Kalyuga & Sweller, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.96.3.558)

**Recommendation:** accept “I know what unemployment means, but I cannot calculate it” as useful starting information. If a consequential distinction remains unclear, offer one representative course question and inspect where the learner would start. Do not require a test when the learner has already described the need adequately.

A topic name is insufficient to select a worked example or formula. The actual question and course conventions must determine the quantity, method, population definitions, and expected answer. This is a constraint on the proposed evaluation, not a claim that the owner's description was inadequate.

### Explained examples and practice deserve selective use

Small algebra experiments found advantages from studying worked examples alongside problems, particularly on structurally similar subsequent tasks. Transfer to different structures required separate examination. This makes an explained calculation a reasonable starting candidate, with success assessed on a new problem rather than recognition of the demonstrated solution. [Sweller & Cooper, 1985](https://onderwijs.felienne.nl/vakdidactiek/materiaal/sweller_worked_examples.pdf)

Retrieval studies with prose found delayed benefits including cued inference. Four worked-example studies, however, did not establish a clear delayed problem-solving advantage for testing over restudy; their pooled interval also did not exclude a useful benefit. The contrast argues for evaluating the actual numerical activity rather than applying a universal rule to quiz frequently. [Butler, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019902), [Van Gog et al., 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9297-3)

A 2025 experiment found promising results from predicting steps in displayed genetics examples, with substantially greater study time and higher attrition in the prompted condition. It did not test passive audio listening. [Van den Broek et al., 2025](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/269108220/1-s2.0-S0959475225001203-main.pdf)

Other experiments found no added benefit from self-explanation over an active conceptual-instruction comparator, or found benefits dependent on the surrounding learning activities. Asking a learner to explain is therefore a candidate technique whose implementation and opportunity cost matter. [Matthews & Rittle-Johnson, 2009](https://web.education.wisc.edu/pmatthews/wp-content/uploads/sites/35/2012/12/matthews-Rittle-Johnson-2009.pdf), [McCarthy & Hinze, 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acp.70093)

**Recommendation:** begin the economics prototype with a well-explained example. Consider a small number of meaningful predictions or explanation opportunities, and a separate new problem with feedback. Compare that with spending the same time on useful explanation or another example. Continuous spoken responses should not become a default obligation merely because they sound like active learning.

### Audio and walking require contextual evaluation

Two classroom experiments found benefits from segmenting a short spoken explanation; results depended on the outcome and comparison. They did not identify an ideal podcast length or evaluate phone equations. [Singh et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.2885)

A repeated-measures study of 61 medical trainees compared seated podcasts, treadmill podcasts, and reading. Its results make walking a credible learning context to investigate, but do not establish general equivalence or the ability to learn unfamiliar calculations while navigating outdoors. [Wolpaw et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9733582/)

**Recommendation:** organize numerical speech around meaningful steps, name quantities consistently, and make it possible to revisit the relevant explanation. Compare a self-contained spoken example with a persistent worked calculation or still graph. Investigate animation only where a changing relationship is the actual difficulty. No reviewed evidence establishes that animation is the best first support for this audience.

The app should establish whether visuals can be consulted, rather than deciding that all walks must become screen-based lessons or that demanding material cannot be studied on a walk. That is a learning-experience choice requiring observation.

### Perceived clarity and demonstrated learning need separate measures

A randomized crossover study in university physics found higher immediate learning scores but lower reported feelings of learning in the active condition. This was a classroom comparison, not an onboarding study. It supports measuring both experience and performance; it does not justify arbitrary inconvenience as educationally beneficial. [Deslauriers et al., 2019](https://dunkin.eeb.ucsc.edu/images/documents/measuring_actual_learning_versus_feeling_of_learning_in_response_to_active_engasgement_in_a_classroom_2019.pdf)

**Recommendation:** ask whether the episode was followable and worthwhile, then observe the task it was intended to support. A fluent explanation is valuable, but “that sounded clear” cannot establish independent calculation ability. Conversely, a demanding interaction that learners abandon has a practical cost even if some completers perform well.

## 3. What to take from N.A.G. and the grilling workflow

N.A.G.'s useful contribution is its distinction between the analytical task and the output format, together with explicit rules for resolving prompt settings. That encourages us to establish what the learner needs before choosing how to present it. Its particular system prompt is oriented toward a dinosaur reference collection and contains a tension between exclusive source grounding and permission to supplement when sources are silent. Its source-count confidence labels would also require independent justification. These should not become SenniBook defaults. [N.A.G. system prompt](https://github.com/lrdmora/N_A_G-Narrative-Anchor-and-Guide/blob/master/prompts/SYSTEM_PROMPT.md)

The audio template offers host formats, brief duration bands, and a prescribed opening/closing structure. Those can inspire presentation options, but they do not settle completeness or learning outcomes. A short duration preset must yield to an explicitly requested complete reading, or the scope must be renegotiated. The template is a workflow example, not evidence of improved learning. [N.A.G. audio template](https://github.com/lrdmora/N_A_G-Narrative-Anchor-and-Guide/blob/master/prompts/PROMPT_AudioOverview.md)

Matt Pocock's locally inspected grilling workflow contributes dependency-aware questioning: resolve a decision before asking questions that depend on it, and investigate available facts independently. Its exhaustive engineering decision tree is a poor stopping rule for this learner experience. Adopt the dependency principle and finish when consequential teaching decisions are settled. The inspected local snapshot is recorded in the [research plan](learning-experience-plan.md#starting-references).

Develop original SenniBook wording. The research prototype does not require copying either project's prompts, treating uploaded source text as instructions, or adding their complete workflows to the app.

## 4. The recommended conversation

Keep five required areas. Their value is the distinction they establish, rather than an empirically privileged count.

| Area | Candidate opening | Decision changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intended outcome | What would you like to be able to understand, explain, or do after listening? | The activity the episode prepares the learner to perform |
| Required coverage | What needs to be included? If you have a list of required topics or learning goals, tell me which file it is. | The scope that cannot silently be omitted and the role of requirements documents |
| Source treatment | Should we explain the material in our own words, read the original wording with explanatory pauses, or use a mixture? | Whether rephrasing is allowed and which original text must be preserved |
| Starting knowledge | What can you already explain or work out without help? | Where instruction should begin, without assuming familiarity equals mastery |
| Needed assistance | What tends to trip you up, or what would you like us to spend extra time on? | The support to propose, including listening circumstances where relevant |

These are original candidate questions, open to revision. First-time preparation should make all five areas clear. An answer that already supplies another area can be acknowledged and confirmed. For returning learners, the proposal is to present the existing five answers for confirmation and update what has changed. That preserves required preparation; its ability to catch stale assumptions still needs evaluation.

The distinction between starting knowledge and assistance may be difficult for users to perceive. The fourth area establishes what the learner can currently do; the fifth concerns the obstacle or emphasis they want help with. If one answer supplies both, accept it rather than demanding two differently worded versions.

### Follow-ups and a stopping rule

A follow-up should resolve an uncertainty that could change the episode or reveal a promise the material cannot support. “Everything,” mixed reading/rephrasing, conflicting duration demands, unclear document roles, and source/commentary language differences are examples. The [candidate conversation](learner-interview.md#when-a-follow-up-earns-its-place) supplies concrete wording and conditions.

Distinguish three situations:

- **Unknown learner knowledge:** a legitimate starting point for teaching. Offer a supported approach or a small diagnostic example.
- **Unresolved learner choice:** propose a concrete interpretation and establish whether it is acceptable, especially where omission or rewriting is involved.
- **Missing source evidence:** disclose what cannot be verified or taught from the supplied material. More preference questions cannot repair an unreadable passage or an absent requirement list.

End with an ordinary-language brief explaining what will be covered, how the source will be treated, what the learner is trying to do, and where help will be concentrated. Give the learner a meaningful chance to correct it. Preparation is sufficient when all five areas are addressed and remaining uncertainty would not materially change those commitments. A learner can explicitly choose a narrower scope, but the app should not continue describing it as complete coverage of the original scope.

### Where I would push back

The strongest version of the owner's proposal is deliberate preparation that catches avoidable misunderstanding. Framing omitted information as human laziness is less useful. A learner may not know what distinction matters, may use a word differently, or may not be able to diagnose their own difficulty. The conversation should make consequential choices understandable and help identify missing knowledge. Pressure to produce a confident answer can defeat that purpose.

I would also resist treating a longer interview as evidence of diligence. More questions can be justified when they expose a real conflict; they can also repeat known information or require learners to design their own pedagogy. The appropriate standard is the correctness and usefulness of the final brief, followed by the quality of the resulting learning experience.

Finally, I would not promise that listening alone will make numerical skills independently usable. It is a plausible teaching channel whose contribution should be assessed. A separate opportunity to attempt a relevant problem lets us discover whether the missing step has been addressed without forcing every walk into an examination.

## 5. Alternatives and scenario inspection

This was a desk inspection of written alternatives and constructed cases. It did not generate episodes, run the prompts against a model, or observe participants. The findings below identify logical vulnerabilities and candidate remedies; they are not measured success rates.

| Alternative | Strongest reason to consider it | Vulnerability or unresolved question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Free-form brief with a concise confirmation | A learner who already supplies the necessary distinctions may communicate efficiently | Missing preferences can remain invisible. Use as a fair evaluation comparator, not an adopted removal of required preparation. |
| B. Five fixed questions, then generate | Predictable and easy to explain; makes important areas visible | Vague or conflicting answers can leave the central decision unresolved after all five have been answered. |
| C. Five required areas, consequential follow-ups, shared brief | Preserves the owner's preparation requirement and responds to ambiguity | Depends on correctly recognizing consequential uncertainty; could become leading or repetitive. Recommended candidate for rehearsal. |
| D. Exhaustive branching interview | May surface unusual preferences and provide a rich brief | Can demand decisions that do not affect this episode, create false precision, and delay useful learning. More detail is not automatically better information. |

The free-form comparator should receive the same available sources and reasonable initial instructions. If it already contains all five decisions, repeating them may add little; the candidate must earn any additional burden by catching mistakes or maintaining an accurate brief. If it lacks information, distinguish the benefit of eliciting that information from the quality of subsequent teaching.

| Constructed input | What the inspection exposed | Candidate response or remaining limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Everything in this literature file; make it detailed” | Coverage is specified but permission to rewrite is still ambiguous | Ask original wording versus every idea. Neither “deep” nor “complete” settles both. |
| “Read the stories exactly, but explain the background” | Added explanation can be mistaken for source text | Establish the source of background and clearly signal commentary. Interview correctness alone cannot verify the eventual reading. |
| “Read the German original; I usually listen in English” | A stored language preference can accidentally become a translation instruction | Confirm the language of original reading and commentary separately. |
| “Use the Roman Empire learning goals” | Mentioning each term could be mistaken for meeting each requirement | Preserve the required operation, such as explaining a relationship, and later inspect its substantive treatment. |
| “I know GDP, but can't do the questions” | Familiarity can cause the numerical steps to be skipped | Accept the reported need; inspect an actual task before choosing a method. |
| “I'm not sure what I don't understand” | Mandatory diagnosis can pressure a novice into guessing | Offer a supported starting point or one representative example. Do not require an articulate self-diagnosis. |
| “Every word, plus explanations, in a short episode” | A format preference conflicts with the content commitment | Propose division into episodes or explicitly negotiate scope. Do not silently compress. |
| “Quote these passages, explain the rest” | A single global reading/paraphrase choice is inadequate | Record treatment by selected section or material type. |
| A required page is unreadable | A polished brief can conceal incomplete evidence | Identify the affected scope; do not claim complete original-word coverage until checked. |
| “Same as last time,” with a changed syllabus | Reuse can preserve a now-wrong requirement list | Surface the source change and confirm the affected brief. |
| “Whatever you think is best” after requesting completeness | Delegation may be mistaken for permission to omit | Recommend a concrete treatment and establish acceptance of its consequences. |
| “I won't look at the phone on a walk” | Optional visuals can become essential without agreement | Make the spoken explanation self-contained and offer any visual separately. Whether it teaches the task sufficiently remains an empirical question. |
| Politics for interest, with no syllabus | An assessment-oriented interview can invent obligations | Establish the exploratory question and evidence boundaries; no external requirements are necessary. |
| The learner changes from summary to original reading midway | Earlier answers can silently override the correction | Update dependent scope and duration decisions, then restate the brief. |

These cases favour C over an unconditional “five answers then generate” rule. They do not show how often actual learners encounter the cases, whether the model will recognize them, or whether the wording will elicit the intended distinctions.

### Recorded review passes and revisions

The needs pass separated coverage, original wording, and the target activity, then checked them against the three reported course situations. The evidence pass challenged blanket claims for longer questioning, frequent retrieval, mandatory self-explanation, and restrictions on walking. The scenario pass exposed conflicting duration, mixed source treatment, stale briefs, and missing evidence as conditions that a fixed completion count cannot resolve.

A separate agent's review of the first candidate wording identified three further defects, corrected in the accompanying candidate:

1. The German example began with the whole specified document but its closing brief referred only to selected stories. The revised brief preserves all material agreed for original reading and requires any different treatment to be explicit.
2. The economics follow-on question only changed the numbers. The revised proposal includes choosing the method on another suitable course question and assessing selection, execution, and interpretation separately.
3. The knowledge and assistance questions could repeat the same diagnosis. The revised knowledge question asks what the learner can already do; the assistance area acknowledges known difficulties and uses the answer to propose support or establish consequential listening circumstances.

These revisions demonstrate changes from analytical review. Actual completion of the conversation, generation behavior, listening, and learning outcomes remain unobserved.

## 6. Evaluation that can establish more than plausibility

Separate three questions: did preparation capture the intended brief, did the resulting episode meet it, and could the learner subsequently perform the intended task? A failure at one stage should not be concealed by success at another.

### Rehearse preparation first

Use an actual course excerpt, a requirements list where relevant, and a representative question with reference guidance. Have the learner state their intended outcome and non-negotiable needs to the facilitator before trying a candidate. This provides a record against which the resulting brief can be checked; it is not something the interviewer can magically infer.

Observe whether each question changes or confirms a consequential decision, how misunderstandings are corrected, what information remains missing, and which repetitions feel unnecessary. Record “I don't know” responses and abandonment rather than examining only complete, confident answers. Avoid teaching participants the desired answer through leading questions.

A small owner/friends round can identify failures and improve wording. It will not justify a universal effect size or a claim that the optimal number of questions has been found. Realistic tasks and iterative rounds are consistent with official user-research guidance, which is methodological guidance rather than evidence of learning improvement. [GOV.UK: planning research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service), [moderated testing](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing)

### Inspect what the episode actually delivers

For annotated reading, compare the selected original text with the script and, later, the spoken output. Check omissions, substitutions, sequence, and the separation of commentary. Do not substitute a model's assertion of completeness for that comparison. For requirements-based teaching, check each required operation and its supporting explanation, rather than counting term mentions.

For economics, select an actual course task before writing the example and the evaluation. Score method selection, relevant data, steps, units, and interpretation separately. A question that supplies the formula cannot assess whether the learner can select it. A numerically altered copy mainly checks a similar application; assessing broader recognition requires an appropriately different question, with reference guidance confirming comparability.

Compare conditions that can answer one question at a time. To evaluate preparation, keep source quality, model, and output opportunity reasonably comparable. To evaluate practice, compare its time with another useful activity. To evaluate visuals, compare audio alone with a persistent calculation or still before adding animation as another variable. Record the unavoidable differences instead of attributing every outcome to the preferred feature.

### Observe use and later performance

Collect immediate followability, effort, interruptions, and willingness to repeat the experience. Then use an independent explanation or calculation task and a later check after a recorded interval. Use parallel tasks and counterbalance order where feasible, recording prior familiarity and intervening study. Real walking conditions should be represented before generalizing from seated rehearsals.

Set the rubric before comparing candidates. Any invented quotation, silent omission of required original text, unsupported substantive claim, or incorrect reference calculation prevents accepting that output as meeting the brief. Question count and enjoyment remain useful observations but cannot compensate for such failures. Exploratory pilot observations should be retained individually, including negative cases, without converting a handful of participants into a precise percentage improvement.

## 7. Decision and outstanding evidence

Proceed to rehearse the candidate conversation with the owner, starting with one real economics exercise and its available answer guidance. Economics is a useful first case because it can reveal whether the interview identifies a task-level difficulty that a definition-focused overview would miss. The literature case should also be inspected with real text before any claim of complete annotated reading.

The current review has established a needs map, inspected primary evidence and counterevidence, compared reference workflows, drafted original questions and follow-up conditions, and examined constructed failure cases. It supports a concrete research candidate. It has not established learner acceptance, diagnostic accuracy, source-complete generation, better retention, or independent calculation performance.

Keep politics, history, and economics teaching prompts as the subsequent collaborative stage. Biology remains open. Subject-specific evidence and representative material are still needed; this preparation review does not count as having completed those curricula or final prompts. No application behavior or production prompt has been changed by this research.

## Source and review notes

This is a focused decision review, not a systematic literature review. Primary studies were selected for their ability to change the preparation or evaluation decisions, including contrary and boundary findings. Original papers or author/institutional copies were used; no raw study data were reanalysed. Full-text access and restricted-access leads for the ten learning studies are documented in [learning-evidence.md](learning-evidence.md).

Additional sources inspected for this memo:

- **Conrad & Schober (1999):** full BLS report, including experiment designs, atypical/typical mappings, clarification costs, and the limits of field reinterview evidence. The separate scanned 1997 article was located but not fully assessed here.
- **Zou et al. (2023):** full institutional preprint, including participant selection, four tasks, prepared question pools, available search alternatives, outcomes, and limitations. Search performance is not a learning outcome.
- **Deslauriers et al. (2019):** full institutional copy, including randomized crossover design, two class sessions, independently prepared immediate tests, and subjective ratings. No delayed podcast outcome is implied.
- **Aliannejadi et al. (2019), [open-domain clarification](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06554):** [full paper](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/getpdf.php?id=1339) inspected as a benchmark caution. The striking best-question result relies on oracle selection in an offline collection. It is not an expected gain from this interview and is not used as evidence of learning improvement.
- **N.A.G.:** the linked system and audio template files inspected on 2026-09-14. Repository content can change; observations refer to those inspected versions. No generated-output validation was performed.
- **Matt Pocock:** local `grilling` and `research` instructions inspected. Their procedural advice was adapted to the user's narrower learning task; the local skill is not a primary learning study.

The work used a separate research agent to inspect learning studies and challenge the draft. Its review is an additional analytical pass, not participant evidence or an independent replication. No research hours are claimed: active effort across the parallel work was not measured. Completion of this bounded review does not imply completion of the subsequent learner evaluations.

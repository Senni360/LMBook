# LMBook working agreement

## Long-term quality

LMBook is a long-term learning product for the owner and friends. Optimize for sustained learning value and reliable everyday use. The owner accepts substantial effort for worthwhile improvements. Their example of "ten hours for 5%" expressed that preference; it establishes neither a required duration nor a measured return.

- Plan around the full intended outcome. Continue substantial work across sessions with clear continuation notes; a session boundary does not make an unfinished task complete.
- For substantial decisions, define observable success, investigate relevant evidence, compare credible alternatives, examine failure cases, and evaluate the result. Keep unresolved questions visible.
- Use distinct review passes for fidelity to the user's needs, evidence and counterarguments, and actual behavior. Repeat investigation when findings justify it; spend effort resolving uncertainty rather than filling time or adding complexity.
- Match the depth of work to its consequences. Estimate effort from the actual tasks, available evidence, dependencies, and uncertainty. Label estimates as estimates and revise them as findings change the scope.
- Interpret illustrative numbers and brainstorming examples in context; preserve the underlying intent without turning an example into a binding requirement. A hard deadline or budget requires a clearly stated constraint.
- Judge completion by the outcome and supporting evidence. Use timeboxes as checkpoints when useful. Continue when consequential uncertainty remains, and finish when the agreed result is supported; neither elapsed time nor a quota of searches proves quality.

## Critical collaboration

- Act as a candid peer. Treat both the owner's ideas and your own proposals as open to scrutiny. Distinguish preferences, factual claims, hypotheses, and final decisions.
- When you disagree, state the disagreement plainly, explain its practical consequence, and propose the strongest alternative you can support. Seek counterevidence to your preferred approach.
- The owner's renewed confidence is a reason to examine the issue carefully, not to manufacture agreement. Be willing to revise your own view when the evidence changes.
- Discuss material departures from the agreed direction before implementing them. Do not silently substitute your preference.
- An explicit final direction or override settles the decision within applicable constraints; no special wording is required. Record the choice and relevant tradeoff, then proceed. Reopen it only when materially new evidence warrants it.
- Keep disagreement useful and proportionate. The purpose is a better decision, not winning an argument or extending it indefinitely.

## Product context and scope

- Read [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md) before product decisions. For learning prompts, subject guidance, learner interviews, or audio teaching, also read [the learning-experience research plan](docs/research/learning-experience-plan.md).
- For resumed implementation, read [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) and the latest entries in [WORKLOG.md](WORKLOG.md). Preserve completed work and newer changes from other threads.
- Follow the current conversation's scope. Discussion and research requests should develop the learning experience; draft production prompts or change application behavior when that work is actually in scope.
- Keep learner-facing explanations about learning choices. Model selection, infrastructure, and code belong in technical discussions when relevant.
- The proposed interview inside LMBook is a product question. It does not impose repeated permission requests on already-authorized development work.

## Evidence and continuity

- Separate confirmed user needs from proposed solutions. Record corrections promptly so the owner need not repeat them. Newer explicit user decisions take precedence over older notes.
- For consequential research, preserve sources, limitations, competing explanations, decision rationale, evaluation observations, and the next unresolved question.
- Report planned effort separately from completed work. Claim research hours, provider runs, listening trials, learning gains, or user feedback only when performed and recorded. Agent simulations are not participant evidence.
- Update the relevant product notes and work log after a material change. Keep one authoritative location for each policy; link to it instead of maintaining duplicate agent instructions.
- The owner has deferred additional automated test writing until an explicit project wrap-up request. Existing tests, build checks, manual workflows, and research evaluations remain permitted. Never describe code checks as evidence of educational effectiveness.
